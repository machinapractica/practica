# Vision

Friends should be able to play Deep Sea Adventure together as a multiplayer web game, each using their own device, while still feeling part of the same shared game.

## Desired experience

Players spend their attention on the game and one another. Each person can understand what is happening, when their participation is needed, and how to make their own choices. The group shares a clear understanding of the game's progress without needing to gather around one screen.

The experience should preserve the appeal of playing Deep Sea Adventure with friends. The browser serves that experience; it should not become the main thing players have to learn or manage.

## Outcomes

- A group of friends can play a complete game together from their own devices.
- Players can follow the shared game and understand the consequences of their choices.
- The group finishes feeling that it played together, with attention available for conversation and shared reactions.

## Principles

**Serve the shared experience.** Judge product choices by whether they help friends enjoy a game together.

**Make play understandable.** Give players enough clarity to act confidently and follow what others have done.

**Respect individual devices.** Each participant should be able to take part through their own browser without depending on another player's screen.

**Keep the game's identity central.** Preserve what the group values about playing Deep Sea Adventure. The exact expectations for adaptation need confirmation.

## Non-goals

This vision does not establish a public matchmaking service, a broader games platform, or a redesigned set of game rules. It does not select technology or define a delivery plan.

## Assumptions and tensions to resolve

The working assumption is that “Deep Sea Adventure” means an adaptation of the existing game. Should the experience reproduce that game faithfully, or are deliberate changes intended?

Friends playing on separate devices may be in the same room or in different places. Which setting matters most remains open; the vision does not assume that the game itself must provide conversation tools.

Clarity in a browser may call for a different presentation from a physical game. Those presentation choices should support the intended game experience, with any changes to play treated as explicit product decisions.
