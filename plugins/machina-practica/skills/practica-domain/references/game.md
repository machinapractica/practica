# Game source checklist

Identify publisher, edition, expansions, errata, and exact page/section references. Account for components, setup, participant roles, ordinary turn, legal choices, scoring, round/match end, ties, exceptional states, and hidden information. Work through one complete ordinary turn and the transition into the next turn. Treat a companion app or prior implementation as secondary evidence unless the user names it authoritative.

Use a source table with claim, location, fact/interpretation/ambiguity, and consequence. Keep copyright-heavy rulebook text out of the summary; write a sourced operational paraphrase. Missing rules or conflicting editions stay unresolved rather than being filled with familiar conventions from another game.
